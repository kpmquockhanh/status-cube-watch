# Pod Enclosure Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** A closed, rain-proof pod for the board plus a 6.2 x 34 x 41 mm cell, with a Garmin-style quarter-turn boss, a desk foot that leaves the Type-C free and a handlebar clamp, as parametric OpenSCAD with geometry and STEP checks.

**Architecture:** `3d-printing/pod.scad` `include`s `board.scad` and models every part where it sits in the pod frame. That frame is board-local with `O = [0,0,0]` and `lean = 0`: x is portrait right, y is back from the glass front, z is portrait up. Parts turn into print orientation only for output. The pod has five parts:

- a **tub**: the rear shell, holding the board, the carrier and the cell, with the boss on its back;
- a **front plate**: the bezel, a flat face with the window and four countersunk screws;
- one flat **TPU gasket** between the plate and the tub, covering both the glass border and the tub rim, which sit in the same plane at y = 0;
- a **carrier plate**: posts press the PCB, pegs locate it, the lead runs in a groove, and the plate rests on ledges of the tub, so the cell never carries the board;
- a tethered **TPU Type-C plug**.

The foot and the clamp share one quarter-turn receiver module. Every check is a `part="check_*"` intersection that `test.sh` expects empty or solid, as in the easel.

**Tech Stack:** OpenSCAD via `3d-printing/tools/openscad` (Docker, `--backend=manifold`), bash `test.sh`, `tools/stlcheck.py`, and `tools/stepcheck.py` (OCP, manifold3d, trimesh).

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-24-pod-design.md`

## Global Constraints

- Leave the wedge (`stand.scad`) and the easel (`easel.scad`) untouched. `./test.sh stand` and `./test.sh easel` still pass.
- `board.scad` gets new facts only, never a changed value. `pod.scad` assigns no variable that `board.scad` assigns.
- Rigid parts are MJF PA12, and the gasket and plug are TPU. Plan on no supports. Use clearances of 0.2 or more between printed parts, and walls of 0.8 or more (1.0 around a pilot hole).
- Use M2 self-tapping screws into Ø1.8 pilots, as in the easel (`pilot_d = 1.8; screw_d = 2.4`). Pod screws are countersunk M2 x 8: 90 deg, head Ø3.8, head height 1.2.
- Default cell: `bat = [41, 34, 6.2]` (length along z, width along x, thickness along y). The buffer `bat_buf` defaults to 5; assert 3 ≤ `bat_buf` ≤ 5. The lead end is `lead_end = "bottom"` or `"top"`.
- `board_flip = false` only. Assert it: the bay position follows the antenna, and only the unflipped case is designed. The IMU still rotates the picture when the pod is mounted upside down.
- Metal (cell, buffer, lead, screws) stays at least `metal_keep = 2` from the antenna footprint in x and z, at every depth. The bay starts `ant_keep = 3` past the antenna.
- Values that come from a reference rather than a measurement are marked `// VERIFY` in a comment and listed in the README: antenna position, every quarter-turn dimension, the BAT header route, and plug interference.
- Exported STLs go in `3d-printing/` as `pod-*.stl`. Every exported part passes `stlcheck.py` (watertight) under an `--max` envelope.

## Review Focus

1. **The real cell has its lead or protection board at the other end, or a shorter buffer.** All checks hold for `lead_end="top"` and for `bat_buf=3`; Tasks 1, 2 and 9 run those variants.
2. **The board sits up to 0.2 mm off nominal in y (MJF tolerance).** The gasket must still be squeezed on the glass and must not bottom out. Task 4 checks `glass_dy = ±0.2`.
3. **The pod goes on the foot or the bar turned 180 deg (chin up or chin down).** The receiver takes both. A plugged-in cable and the open plug flap must clear the foot either way; Task 7 checks both `pod_turn = 0` and `pod_turn = 180`.
4. **Vibration on the bike turns the boss towards unlocked.** The detent must resist that turn, and the hooks must keep the boss from lifting at the locked angle. Task 6 checks that the detent interferes at 70 deg, sits free at 90 deg, and that the boss is retained when lifted 0.5 at 90 deg.
5. **A screw hole lets water in.** Each screw passes through the gasket with at least `seal_land = 1.0` of gasket all round, between the hole and both the cavity and the skirt. Task 4 checks this, with a control.

### Task 6: Quarter-turn boss and receiver

**Files:** Modify `3d-printing/pod.scad` and `3d-printing/test.sh`.

**Interfaces:**
- Consumes `tub()` and `back_y`.
- Produces quarter-turn parameters, all `// VERIFY`. First look for a published Garmin quarter-turn drawing (WebSearch) and cite it in the comment. Without one, use these starting values, and say so in the comment:

  | Parameter | Value | What it is |
  |---|---|---|
  | `qt_neck_d` | 20.0 | boss core under the tabs |
  | `qt_tab_r` | 13.5 | tab outer radius |
  | `qt_tab_w` | 9.0 | tab chord |
  | `qt_tab_t` | 2.4 | tab thickness |
  | `qt_gap` | 2.6 | from the back face to the tab underside, where the hooks go |
  | `qt_hook_t` | 2.2 | hook thickness |
  | `qt_clr` | 0.2 | clearance |
  | `qt_base_d` | 31 | receiver outer |
  | `qt_detent` | 0.4 | bump height |

  `qt_lock = 90`: the boss is inserted with the pod landscape and locks with the pod portrait.
- `qt_boss()`, pod frame: a neck and two tabs at ±x of the boss axis when locked, on the back face at (`glass_b/2`, `back_y`, pod centre z). It has two detent notches at the locked angle. The tub gets the boss in `tub()`.

- `qt_mount(a, lift)`: places the tub's boss into the receiver frame, turned by `a` deg from insert and lifted by `lift`.
- Parts:
  - `check_qt(a, lift)` = boss ∩ receiver, reading `-D qt_a=… -D qt_lift=…`;
  - `check_qt_detent` = the detent bump alone ∩ boss;
  - `qt_test_boss` (a 36 x 36 x 4 plate carrying the boss);
  - `qt_test_receiver`.
